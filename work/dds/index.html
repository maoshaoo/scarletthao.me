Defense Digital Service · 2022–2024

Crossing the Valley of Death

Most promising defense software dies somewhere between a successful pilot and permanent funding. I led a dual-use small drone program through that stretch, from a proof of concept the community loved to a dependable product with a long-term home in the U.S. Navy.

BeforeProof of conceptFound product-market fit fast. But every install was a gamble.
AfterDependable, permanent3,500 operators. A home in the Navy.

The situation

Cheap, Chinese-made commercial drones had become a real threat to U.S. operations. Our program started as a proof of concept in DDS’s counter-drone portfolio, and it caught fire fast within the community!

But demand had outrun the product and the team, and the community was feeling it. Installing it could brick the hardware or fail to deliver what we promised, with no telling which you’d get, and DDS didn’t have the budget to carry the program on its own.

What I did

  1. 1

    Made it dependable

    I grew the team to ten, adding QA and technical writing,1 and went after the friction breaking operators’ trust, like installation and onboarding. Our BHAG2: get every operator to fly their first mission successfully within 15 minutes.

  2. 2

    Grew it like a consumer product

    I sat with our users to get smart on their world and how they used our product. We stitched data instrumentation together across key journeys so we could paint the picture with numbers too.

    Along the way, I worked the program into the waiver approvals units needed to operate, and once we were in a good place, I made our roadmap public so operators knew what was coming and when.

  3. 3

    Got it a permanent home

    When DDS couldn’t carry the program alone, I rallied the counter-drone community behind it,3 and they answered, overwhelmingly. Meanwhile, I found creative ways to keep us funded, including a new paid offering,4 and the team secured an interim Authority to Operate, with a path to the Navy.

All of this while our contract was recompeted and we lived through a string of near government shutdowns!5

What I learned

Your customer is your biggest advocate. Without the community’s overwhelming response, a mission-critical program would have died in the valley.

Use your voice & take a stand in what you believe. Service members at home and abroad relied on it for mission-critical purposes. All I did was amplify their stories.

A small team can do a lot. Ten people and $2M went a long way.

I’m really proud of what this team did for our community, and national security at large.
