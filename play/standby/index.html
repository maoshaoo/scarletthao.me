How it’s builtOne web page, public data and a phone that reads screenshots.
The app
One page of plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No framework, no server, no account. It can be added to a phone’s home screen like an app.
Reading screenshots
Text recognition runs on the phone with Tesseract.js, then a parser picks out each flight, its Polaris seats and the standby list. Optionally, Claude reads it instead, with your own API key.
How full flights run
Public U.S. DOT data (BTS T-100): seats and passengers on United’s routes, by month. A GitHub Action refreshes it on the 3rd of each month.
The forecast
Day of week, holiday peaks, how full the route runs, how many flights there are, and your own past checks. A trip counts only as good as its weaker direction.
Good time to go
A hand-written list of when 52 places are at their best, matched against months when their flights have room both ways.
Your data
Your log, places and settings stay in this browser. You can sync to your own private Google Sheet.
Made with
Claude Code, over a weekend. Source on GitHub.
